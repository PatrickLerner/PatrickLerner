excerpt: Istanbul feels overpriced and some of it is an outright scam. The good part of the city is free. A long list, every entry linked to Google Maps.

Istanbul feels overpriced the moment you arrive, and a few of the headline attractions are an outright scam.

Hagia Sophia is the clearest case. It is a working mosque. Turks and worshippers walk in for nothing. Foreign tourists get routed upstairs and charged around €22 for the privilege. Chora is €20 now and the Museum Pass no longer covers it. The Bosphorus cruise boats sold at Eminönü charge many times an İstanbulkart fare for a loop with no stops. Çamlıca Tower charges for a view you can get for free from the hill next to it.

None of that is the city. The good part of Istanbul is free, and it is not the leftovers: the best view, the best tile work, the best forest, the oldest market and half the modern art are all free. Everything below costs nothing to enter, not just to look at from outside, unless noted.

## When to go

**March and April.** Far fewer tourists, mild weather instead of brutal heat. April brings the tulips at Emirgan Park.

Aim for Ramadan if the dates line up. It moves 11 days earlier each year, so it is drifting out of this window: roughly 7 Feb to 8 Mar 2027, then 27 Jan to 25 Feb 2028. In 2027 the last days of Ramadan fall in early March. That is the sweet spot.

Ramadan is a reason to come, not a problem. Restaurants stay open all day. Nobody expects you to fast. And the city gets two things it has no other time of year: _mahya_, light messages strung between the minarets, and the huge shared iftar in Sultanahmet Square at sunset. Bring a jacket, it is cool.

## Where to stay

**Kadıköy or Balat.** Both sit close to everything without sitting in the middle of the crowds. Skip Sultanahmet and Taksim as bases.

[Kadıköy](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Kad%C4%B1k%C3%B6y,+Istanbul) has ferries to most places you want to go, including the Princes' Islands, and all the Asian side sights on your doorstep. To cross to the old city, take the Marmaray under the Bosphorus: two stops, no fuss.

[Balat](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Balat,+Istanbul) is a few stops on the Golden Horn tram from the city center. Quiet streets, good cafés, and the Fener and Golden Horn sights are a walk away.

## Mosques

All free, all open between prayer times. Dress modestly, women cover hair.

**[Blue Mosque](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Sultanahmet+Camii)** (Sultanahmet). 17th century, 20,000 blue Iznik tiles, six minarets. 8:30 to 18:00.

**[Süleymaniye Mosque](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=S%C3%BCleymaniye+Camii)**. Mimar Sinan's best work, on the highest hill. The best free view in the city: Galata Tower, the Bosphorus, the Golden Horn. 9:00 to 18:00.

**[Little Hagia Sophia](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=K%C3%BC%C3%A7%C3%BCk+Ayasofya+Camii)** (St. Sergius & Bacchus). A 6th-century Byzantine church, far quieter than the real one. Garden café with cats. 10:00 to 18:30.

**[Ortaköy Mosque](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Ortak%C3%B6y+Camii)**. Baroque, right on the water. Best at sunset with the lit bridge behind it.

**[Rüstem Pasha Mosque](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=R%C3%BCstem+Pa%C5%9Fa+Camii)**. Hidden above the Spice Bazaar, up a stairway. The richest Iznik tile work in the city. Tiny, and rarely busy.

**[New Mosque](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Yeni+Cami+Emin%C3%B6n%C3%BC)** (Yeni Cami). On the Eminönü waterfront next to the Spice Bazaar and the ferry piers. Huge courtyard, pigeons, always busy with locals.

**[Eyüp Sultan Mosque](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Ey%C3%BCp+Sultan+Camii)**. The holiest Islamic site in Istanbul. Almost all locals and pilgrims, few tourists. Walk up through the hillside graveyard to [Pierre Loti](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Piyer+Loti+Tepesi) for a free Golden Horn view. The cable car costs money, the walk does not.

**[Şakirin Mosque](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%C5%9Eakirin+Camii)** (Üsküdar). Modern. Zeynep Fadıllıoğlu designed the inside, the first woman ever to do so for a mosque. Next to Karacaahmet Cemetery.

**[Mihrimah Sultan Mosque](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Mihrimah+Sultan+Camii+Edirnekap%C4%B1)** (Edirnekapı). Sinan again, on the highest of the seven hills. 161 windows. It floods with light at midday.

**[Çamlıca Mosque](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%C3%87aml%C4%B1ca+Camii)** (Asian side). The largest mosque in Turkey, up on Çamlıca Hill. The terrace may be the best free view in the city.

## Markets and streets

**[Grand Bazaar](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Kapal%C4%B1%C3%A7ar%C5%9F%C4%B1)**. Over 4,000 shops, one of the world's oldest covered markets. 8:30 to 19:00. Haggle if you buy. Looking is free.

**[Spice Bazaar](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=M%C4%B1s%C4%B1r+%C3%87ar%C5%9F%C4%B1s%C4%B1)** (Egyptian Bazaar). Smaller and prettier than the Grand Bazaar. Spices, teas, Turkish delight. 8:00 to 19:00.

**[İstiklal Avenue](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%C4%B0stiklal+Caddesi)**. The main shopping street in Beyoğlu. Cars banned, old tram down the middle, street music and cafés.

**[Taksim Square](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Taksim+Meydan%C4%B1)**. Urban heart, Republic Monument, main metro hub.

**[Book Bazaar](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Sahaflar+%C3%87ar%C5%9F%C4%B1s%C4%B1)** (Sahaflar Çarşısı). A courtyard of book sellers between the Grand Bazaar and Beyazıt Mosque. Here since Ottoman times.

**[Arasta Bazaar](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Arasta+%C3%87ar%C5%9F%C4%B1s%C4%B1+Sultanahmet)**. Small arcade behind the Blue Mosque, built to pay for it. Calmer than the Grand Bazaar, and nobody pushes you to buy.

**[Fish Market](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Bal%C4%B1k+Pazar%C4%B1+Beyo%C4%9Flu)** (Balık Pazarı), Beyoğlu. Just off İstiklal. Mussels, pickles, fish stalls. Worth a walk through even if you buy nothing.

**[Kadıköy Tuesday Market](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Kad%C4%B1k%C3%B6y+Sal%C4%B1+Pazar%C4%B1)** (Salı Pazarı). Huge local street market on the Asian side. Fruit, veg, cloth. No tourists at all.

**[Fatih Wednesday Market](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Fatih+%C3%87ar%C5%9Famba+Pazar%C4%B1)** (Çarşamba Pazarı). The most religious and the most real of the big local markets.

**[Akmar Pasajı](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Akmar+Pasaj%C4%B1+Kad%C4%B1k%C3%B6y)** (Kadıköy). Basement arcade of record shops, used books, band shirts and metal posters. Home of the Istanbul punk and metal scene since the 80s. Free to browse.

## Neighborhoods

**[Balat](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Balat,+Istanbul)**. Painted houses, cobbled streets, the old Jewish quarter, trendy cafés. Everyone is here for photos.

**[Merdivenli Yokuş](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Merdivenli+Yoku%C5%9F+Balat)** (Balat). The stair street with the row of painted houses. The Balat photo. Get there early, it fills up.

**[Cihangir](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Cihangir,+Istanbul)** and **[Çukurcuma](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%C3%87ukurcuma,+Istanbul)**. Hill streets off Taksim with a Montmartre feel. Odd little cafés, antique shops.

**[Galata](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Galata,+Istanbul)**. Art galleries and design shops. The tower costs money but looks great from below.

**[Kadıköy](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Kad%C4%B1k%C3%B6y,+Istanbul)** (Asian side). Take the ferry, about €1.50. Local life, the [Moda](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Moda,+Kad%C4%B1k%C3%B6y) district, cheaper food.

**[Kuzguncuk](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Kuzguncuk,+Istanbul)** (Asian side). Wooden Ottoman houses, quieter than Balat, fewer tourists. Two specific stops on the hillside, 150m apart: the [painted wooden houses](https://maps.app.goo.gl/hPiuVp753FWCroKF9) themselves, and the [Bosphorus view point](https://maps.app.goo.gl/BpV5v9TthnCTrLWx8) just downhill.

**[Fener](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Fener,+Istanbul)**. Next to Balat. Home of the Ecumenical Patriarchate, free to enter, and the red brick [Phanar Greek College](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%C3%96zel+Fener+Rum+Lisesi) on the hill above.

**[Zeyrek](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Zeyrek,+Istanbul)**. Steep Ottoman backstreets below the Valens Aqueduct.

**[Arnavutköy](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Arnavutk%C3%B6y,+Istanbul)** and **[Bebek](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Bebek,+Istanbul)**. Two Bosphorus villages with wooden _yalı_ mansions. A seaside path links them, 20 minutes on foot.

**[Üsküdar](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%C3%9Csk%C3%BCdar,+Istanbul)** (Asian side). Ferry from Eminönü. Old center packed with mosques. [Maiden's Tower](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=K%C4%B1z+Kulesi) sits just offshore.

## Parks and outdoor spaces

**[Gülhane Park](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=G%C3%BClhane+Park%C4%B1)**. Former imperial garden near Sultanahmet. Best in spring for the tulips. 6:00 to 22:30.

**[Galata Bridge](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Galata+K%C3%B6pr%C3%BCs%C3%BC)**. Walk across for Golden Horn views, especially at sunset. Locals fishing off both sides.

**[Karaköy waterfront](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Karak%C3%B6y+Sahil)** and the promenade beneath Topkapı Palace. Bosphorus views the whole way.

**[Galataport](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Galataport)**. 1km of waterfront, modern shops, the Istanbul Modern area. Open 24/7.

**[Emirgan Park](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Emirgan+Korusu)**. Biggest tulip display in the city during the April festival. Three Ottoman pavilions, Bosphorus views.

**[Yıldız Park](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Y%C4%B1ld%C4%B1z+Park%C4%B1)**. Former palace grounds above Beşiktaş, wooded and steep. Quiet on weekdays.

**[Fethi Paşa Korusu](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Fethi+Pa%C5%9Fa+Korusu)** (Asian side). Hillside grove above Üsküdar looking straight across at the old city. Sunset spot locals use.

**[Çamlıca Hill](https://maps.app.goo.gl/bChBHxccEczQgBFr8)**. Highest hill over the city center. Free viewing terraces next to the mosque, both continents in one frame.

**[Aydos Forest and Aydos Castle](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Aydos+Kalesi)** (Sancaktepe). The true high point of Istanbul at 537m. Walk up through pine forest, then a short steep scramble to a ruined Byzantine fort on top. No fence, no ticket booth. On a clear day you see the Marmara, the Princes' Islands and the Black Sea. It is far out on the Asian side: metro to Sancaktepe, then a taxi or a long walk. [Aydos Millet Bahçesi](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Aydos+Millet+Bah%C3%A7esi) at the foot of the hill has a city-run _sosyal tesis_ restaurant. Cheap and good.

**[Sarayburnu Park](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%C4%B0BB+Sarayburnu+Park%C4%B1)**. The tip of the old city below Topkapı, where the Golden Horn, the Bosphorus and the Marmara all meet. The Asian side, Maiden's Tower and the bridges in one frame. Runs into Gülhane Park.

**[Moda shoreline](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=40.980187,29.020600)** (Kadıköy). Walk the coast path around the Moda point. Benches, tea gardens, swimmers, the old city skyline across the water. Best at sunset.

**[Sunset terrace above Kasımpaşa](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=41.033926,28.974072)** (Beyoğlu). Five minutes north of Pera Museum. The hill drops away toward the Golden Horn.

**[Belgrad Forest](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Belgrad+Orman%C4%B1)** (Belgrad Ormanı). Real forest an hour north of the city, near Bahçeköy. Marked trails and a running loop. The Ottoman _bentler_ still stand in the trees: stone dams and aqueducts that once fed the city its water. Free on foot, cars pay at the gate. [Picnic area by the stream](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=41.179471,28.960610). The best half day out of Istanbul that is still Istanbul.

**[Güzelce Kemeri](https://maps.app.goo.gl/1tDYf3dNfVi4WZ9U6)** (Güzelcekemer). A Sinan aqueduct from the 1560s, part of Süleyman's Kırkçeşme system that carried water to the city from these hills. It stands in open country near Kemerburgaz. No gate, no ticket, usually nobody there. Two more big Sinan aqueducts, Mağlova and Uzunkemer, sit nearby.

**[Bird Observation Tower](https://maps.app.goo.gl/wDMUeukYtuKNzwX4A)** (near Rumeli Fortress). A National Parks lookout on the hill above the Bosphorus narrows. Istanbul sits on a main bird migration route, and hundreds of thousands of storks and birds of prey pass right over this spot each September and again in spring. The fortress costs money. The tower does not.

## Princes' Islands

The ferry is the only cost. Boats leave from Kabataş, Eminönü, Kadıköy and Bostancı. The Kabataş boat calls at all four islands in order and takes about 90 minutes. Bostancı is much faster if you are already on the Asian side.

No cars on any of the four. You walk, rent a bike, or take an electric shuttle.

**[Büyükada](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada)**. The biggest and the busiest. Walk up to the [Aya Yorgi monastery](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Aya+Yorgi+Kilisesi+B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada) on the south hill. Steep, about 45 minutes, and the best view of the islands. Whole streets of old wooden mansions.

**[Heybeliada](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Heybeliada)**. Pine forest and the best swimming. The [Halki Greek Orthodox school](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Heybeliada+Ruhban+Okulu) sits on the north hill. It has been shut since 1971, but the grounds and the walk up are worth it. Naval academy down on the shore.

**[Burgazada](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Burgazada)**. The quietest of the four. The [Sait Faik Abasıyanık Museum](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Sait+Faik+Abas%C4%B1yan%C4%B1k+M%C3%BCzesi) is the writer's own house. His mother's will made it free in 1959 and it always will be. Wed to Sun, shut Mon and Tue, and sources disagree on the hours. [Kalpazankaya](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Kalpazankaya+Burgazada) on the far side is the sunset spot.

**[Kınalıada](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=K%C4%B1nal%C4%B1ada)**. Closest to the city and the barest, with reddish rock and few trees. Mostly Armenian, with an Armenian church. Good for a short trip and a sunset rather than a hike.

## Historical sites

Free to walk up to, free to view.

**[Egyptian Obelisk of Theodosius](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Dikilita%C5%9F+Sultanahmet)**. Over 3,000 years old, in the Hippodrome square in front of the Blue Mosque.

**[Hippodrome](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Sultanahmet+Meydan%C4%B1)** (Sultanahmet Square). More than the obelisk: the bronze Serpent Column from Delphi, the Walled Obelisk and the German Fountain, all in one square.

**[Valens Aqueduct](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Bozdo%C4%9Fan+Kemeri)**. 4th-century Roman, well preserved, impressive scale.

**[Marmara Sea Walls](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=41.001334,28.938099)**. Byzantine sea walls along the south shore, running west from Sarayburnu past Kumkapı and Samatya. Ruined, no fence, free to walk right up to. Far emptier than the land walls.

**[Theodosian Land Walls](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%C4%B0stanbul+Kara+Surlar%C4%B1)**. 5th century, 6.5km from the Golden Horn to the Marmara. Walk the stretch at Edirnekapı or Yedikule. You can climb parts of it. Watch your footing.

**[Column of Constantine](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%C3%87emberlita%C5%9F+S%C3%BCtunu)** (Çemberlitaş). Put up in 330 AD when Constantinople was founded. Still stands by the tram line, wrapped in iron hoops.

**[Molla Zeyrek Mosque](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Molla+Zeyrek+Camii)**. Once the Pantokrator Monastery, built in the 12th century. After Hagia Sophia it is the biggest Byzantine church still standing here. Restored, free, and almost empty. Good Golden Horn view from the terrace behind it.

**[Hagia Sophia](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Ayasofya)**. Paid entry, around €22, but the exterior is free from Sultanahmet Park. Muslims enter free during prayer.

**[Chora](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Kariye+Camii)** (Kariye Mosque). World-class Byzantine mosaics. Not free any more: €20 for foreign visitors since 2024, and the Museum Pass does not work. Shut to tourists around Friday prayers.

**[Dolmabahçe Palace](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Dolmabah%C3%A7e+Saray%C4%B1)**. Paid interior, but the gate, clock tower and seafront are free to admire.

## Churches

**[St. Anthony of Padua](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=St.+Antuan+Kilisesi+%C4%B0stiklal)**. Off İstiklal, red brick, Venetian-style balcony.

**[Church of St. Stephen of the Bulgars](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Bulgar+Kilisesi+Balat)**. 19th-century Bulgarian Orthodox, cast iron, Byzantine and Ottoman blend. 9:00 to 17:00.

**[Ecumenical Patriarchate](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Fener+Rum+Patrikhanesi)** (St. George's, Fener). The seat of the Orthodox church worldwide. A small building that matters enormously. Airport-style security at the gate.

**[Hagia Triada](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Aya+Triada+Kilisesi+Taksim)** (Taksim). The largest Greek Orthodox church in Istanbul. Dome and twin bell towers, right off Taksim Square.

**[Surp Krikor Lusavoriç](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Surp+Krikor+Lusavori%C3%A7+Kilisesi+Karak%C3%B6y)** (Karaköy). The oldest Armenian church in the city, hidden in a courtyard off the main street.

## Free museums and galleries

**[SALT Galata](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=SALT+Galata)** (Karaköy). The old Ottoman Bank head office. Modern art shows upstairs, the Ottoman Bank Museum down in the vault. There is also a [SALT Beyoğlu](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=SALT+Beyo%C4%9Flu) on İstiklal.

**[Türkiye İş Bankası Museum](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=T%C3%BCrkiye+%C4%B0%C5%9F+Bankas%C4%B1+M%C3%BCzesi)** (Bahçekapı, Fatih). Pink building on Bankacılar Caddesi, in the back street behind the New Mosque. Built as a post office in 1890, a bank branch until 2004, a museum since 2007. Vaults, safes, early banknotes, and the money history of the young republic. Tue to Sun 10:00 to 18:00, shut Monday.

**[Müze Gazhane](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=M%C3%BCze+Gazhane)** (Kadıköy). An old gasworks turned into a cultural site. The Climate Museum and the Cartoon Museum are both in here, plus a science center, galleries, a library and courtyards. Easily half a day, and none of it costs anything.

**[Ara Güler Museum](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Ara+G%C3%BCler+M%C3%BCzesi)** (Bomonti). The archive of Turkey's greatest photographer, in the old Bomonti brewery. Shows change as they work through the archive. Tue to Sat 10:00 to 18:00, Sun 12:00 to 18:00, shut Monday.

**[Atatürk Museum](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Atat%C3%BCrk+M%C3%BCzesi+%C5%9Ei%C5%9Fli)** (Şişli). The house Atatürk lived in before Gallipoli, kept as it was. Small and quick. Run by the city.

**[Abdülmecid Efendi Pavilion](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Abd%C3%BClmecid+Efendi+K%C3%B6%C5%9Fk%C3%BC)** (Bağlarbaşı, Üsküdar). Free, and packed because of it. The home and painting studio of the last Ottoman caliph, who was a serious painter himself. Now restored and used for art shows. Go on a weekday morning or expect a queue.

**[Üsküdar Hanım Sultanlar Müzesi](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Han%C4%B1m+Sultanlar+M%C3%BCzesi+%C3%9Csk%C3%BCdar)**. A museum about the Ottoman royal women, on the Üsküdar waterfront.

**[Casa Botter](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Casa+Botter)** (İstiklal Cd. 235, Beyoğlu). The first Art Nouveau building in Istanbul, put up around 1900 by Raimondo D'Aronco for Jean Botter, tailor to Sultan Abdülhamid II. Left to rot for decades, restored by İBB, now an art space. Worth going in for the building alone. Tue to Sun 10:00 to 19:00, shut Monday. Some shows run later.

**[Gülhane Cistern](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=G%C3%BClhane+Sarn%C4%B1c%C4%B1)** (Gülhane Sanat). A Byzantine cistern under Gülhane Park, shut for years, restored and opened as an art space in 2023. Same lighting treatment as the Basilica Cistern, but empty of tour groups and free. A walkway runs through it. Tue to Sun, roughly 10:00 to 18:00, shut Monday. Sources disagree on the closing time, and a special show may charge.

**[Istanbul Railway Museum](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Sirkeci+Gar%C4%B1)** (Sirkeci Station). Closed at the time of writing. Worth checking before you go: it sits inside the old Orient Express terminus, and it was free.

## Golden Horn art cluster

These sit in a line along the Golden Horn, linked by the tram or a walk. All free, all run by the city. One afternoon covers them.

**[İstanbul Sanat Museum](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Hali%C3%A7+Tersanesi+%C4%B0stanbul+Sanat)** (Haliç Tersanesi). The big one. Istanbul's first public modern art museum, opened in 2024 inside the 600-year-old imperial shipyard that Mehmed II founded. Big shows, a performance space, workshops and a restaurant.

**[Haliç Sanat 1, 2 and 3](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Hali%C3%A7+Sanat+Fener+Evleri)** (Fener Evleri). Three restored 18th-century Fener mansions on the shore, now galleries of the İstanbul Sanat Museum. A few minutes apart on foot. Tue to Sun 10:00 to 17:00, shut Monday. Abdül Ezel Paşa Cd. No:4 and No:95, Mürsel Paşa Cd. No:6.

**[ArtIstanbul Feshane](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=ArtIstanbul+Feshane)**. An old Ottoman cloth factory further up the Horn. Big modern art shows.

Three more that are free only at certain times: **[Pera Museum](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Pera+M%C3%BCzesi)** (Beyoğlu) is free for everyone Friday 18:00 to 22:00 and for all students on Wednesdays, plus Friends of Pera and disabled visitors with one helper. Orientalist paintings, including "The Tortoise Trainer." **[Sakıp Sabancı Museum](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Sak%C4%B1p+Sabanc%C4%B1+M%C3%BCzesi)** (Emirgan) is free every Wednesday. **[Istanbul Modern](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%C4%B0stanbul+Modern)** (Galataport) is free for ages 18 to 25 on Tuesdays, 10:00 to 14:00.

Free entry times change. Check saltonline.org, peramuzesi.org.tr and the other museum sites before you plan a day around them.

## Experiences

**Pet the cats.** Istanbul's street cats are everywhere, clean, and loved by locals.

**Ferry to the Asian side.** About €1.50, and it feels like a mini Bosphorus cruise. Best cheap activity in the city.

**Do not book a "Bosphorus cruise."** The tour boats sold at Eminönü, Kabataş and by the hotels are a rip-off. You pay many times the real price for a loop with no stops and loud music. Take the normal city ferry up the Bosphorus instead. It costs a plain İstanbulkart fare, about 90% less, and it stops at more piers. You can get off, eat, and catch a later boat back. Same water, same view, same bridges.

**Maiden's Tower views.** The tower itself is paid, but the views from the Asian shore are free, especially at sunset.

**[Tünel](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=T%C3%BCnel+Karak%C3%B6y)**. The short underground railway from Karaköy up to Beyoğlu, open since 1875 and the second oldest in the world. One stop, one İstanbulkart fare. Worth riding once or twice, mostly so you do not have to walk up that hill.

## Not free, but worth it

**[Nevmekan Sahil](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Nevmekan+Sahil+%C3%9Csk%C3%BCdar)** (Üsküdar). Municipal library and café on the waterfront in a restored building. Walking in and looking around costs nothing, and the food and tea are cheap and good.

**[Yakamoz Pastanesi](https://maps.app.goo.gl/hsDuqt71jpfavUqm6)** (Kadıköy). Best baklava.

**[Afsona Özbek Restaurant](https://maps.app.goo.gl/ZGy4rs4JniqDSSsu7)** (Kadıköy). Best kebab in the city, and it isn't Turkish. Uzbek.

**[İBB Küçük Çamlıca Sosyal Tesisi](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Wheccyov5HuRXFwd9)**. Cheap, good municipal restaurant with the view. It sits on Küçük Çamlıca next to Çamlıca Tower, which is a different hill from Büyük Çamlıca with the mosque and the free terraces. Don't mix them up. The tower itself is overpriced. Skip it and eat here instead.
